# MK-386 | High-Value Member Selection

For ordinary chat, upload `packs/mk-386.md`, which includes the shared rules, this method, and required references

## Purpose

This skill delivers: **Candidates assessed jointly on frequency, amount, and recency**

Do not rank RFM results from different observation periods as if they shared one basis; separately verify the purpose and authorization for any list export or use

## Required inputs


Available raw records: approved RFM mapping, orders and refunds, contribution, intended benefits and costs, contact eligibility, and product cycles

## Method Steps

1. Use the brand's RFM mapping, valid-order/refund rules, and intended benefit to find loyal, lapsed-loyal, and high-potential candidates. Without a mapping, report raw RFM rather than inventing a weighted total
2. Separate high revenue, actual high contribution, current need, and contact eligibility. A large basket with high returns or fulfillment costs does not automatically justify the most expensive benefit
3. Retention prioritizes members qualifying under existing rules with a relevant need and contact eligibility. Winback targets previously loyal members overdue under the applicable cycle; unresolved complaints call for service first
4. Compare available contribution and benefit costs by person/group, and check the total budget. Without costs, deliver candidates and allocation formulas and request only the benefit cost affecting eligibility
5. Re-evaluate inclusion after refunds, contact eligibility, or cycle corrections. Keep official membership tiers separate from campaign eligibility rather than rewriting policy for one activity

## Calculation and Interpretation

Recalculate R/F/M under existing definitions. Campaign cost = group headcount × per-person benefit and fulfillment cost + fixed costs. State the horizon of historical contribution; unknown future incremental purchases are not guaranteed spending capacity

Check example (hypothetical): A has annual revenue of 20,000 but only 200 contribution after refunds and costs; B has 10,000 revenue and 3,000 contribution. A provided benefit rule requiring at least 1,000 contribution selects B. Correcting A's refunds raises contribution to 2,000 and restores eligibility
