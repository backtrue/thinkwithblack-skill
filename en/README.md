## Your product is ready. What should you do first?

You have a product and want to start selling it. People suggest building a website, running social channels, filming videos, buying ads, or finding retail channels, and every option takes time and money. You need to decide: who should you sell to first, what would make them consider your product, and what single task should you handle today

You can start directly with “I have this product, and I do not know my next step.” Bring your product photos, specifications, or product page, and explain where things stand. If you already have customer inquiries or orders, bring them too; if you are still preparing to launch, start from the product itself and the market you want to reach

At this stage, you need a business partner that works through the task with you: reviewing the information on hand, organizing your options, giving a reason for what to do first, and completing that piece of work. You finish knowing what to prepare today, how to put the result to use, and how to check incoming orders against your spending after you run it

## Put practical marketing methods to work with your AI on real business problems

**ThinkWithBlack Skills** turns the creator's years of hands-on marketing and teaching experience into method packs. This AI business partner provides three things:

- **Identify what to do first from your product and current situation**: organize the data you already have, compare your available options, and propose a first action with the reasoning behind it
- **Apply cross-market practical experience to your business**: draw on experience from work across Taiwan, China, and Japan to factor your customers, sales channels, and transaction terms into the plan
- **Produce the work with you**: once you choose today's task, complete evidence-backed product claims, audience candidates, content drafts, or marketing plans, along with instructions on how to use them and what to check

Adding these Skills gives the AI the complete methods, data requirements, and verification rules in this collection. Your product and market data determine how far this session can go; the method makes sure each step produces something concrete

## Start with the product in your hands and find one step you can finish today

Suppose you are preparing to sell a travel tumbler. You have photos, specifications, and a price, and you are deciding whether to build a website first or run ads. You can hand that material to the AI and say where you are hesitating

If you are still organizing how to introduce the product to customers, both a website and an ad will need those facts. Start with [Product facts](packs/mk-001.md) to verify which specifications can be cited and which claims still need evidence. After reading the method, the AI organizes the facts you already have so you know what you can reliably use to introduce the tumbler

If you then want to know which feature is worth leading with, choose [Unique selling points](packs/mk-002.md). In the fictional exercise included with the materials, two tumblers list weights of 280g and 320g, so “40g lighter than this comparison product” is a claim you can verify against the specs. Whether it belongs as the main message still depends on who you want to serve, how they use the cup, and why they choose one

When you answer “I do not know what customers care about yet,” the method tells the AI to use your existing evidence to list candidate angles and ways to verify them, explain what to check first and why, and show what you can complete after that check. You can choose from those candidates and turn a vague question like “How do I sell this?” into one concrete task on your desk

This collection works like a **marketing medical center**: methods from different disciplines live in one [method index](INDEX.md) so the AI can choose based on the problem in front of you. You upload the full text of the named method pack and complete one task at a time; when it is done, bring the next question to work on pricing, promotion, search, or repeat purchases

## Change the market, and bring in that market's customers and conditions

The creator of these methods has worked across markets in Taiwan, China, and Japan, spanning ecommerce, physical retail channels, brand consulting, and teaching. That cross-market experience forms the background of this collection, with details available on the [personal website](https://bio.thinkwithblack.com/)

In your own business, that means treating the target market as part of every judgment. Who you plan to sell to, which alternatives customers compare, which channel they buy through, what currency they pay in, and what delivery and after-sales terms apply all shape the approach you take

When you take the same product into another market, you can reuse the methods for organizing product facts, understanding demand, and checking costs, while swapping in local data:

| Local market data you bring in | What it helps you decide |
|---|---|
| Local customer inquiries, buying reasons, and comparison products | Which product feature to lead with, along with the reason and supporting evidence |
| Planned sales channels, payment methods, delivery terms, and after-sales rules | What customers need to see before buying and what to add to your product descriptions |
| Local selling price, currency, platform fees, and other costs | How revenue and expenses work out per order and how much you can put toward promotion |

For example, the same delivery promise needs to be checked against the service area and shipping times of the region where you actually sell; the same price needs to be recalculated with that market's fee structure. You can apply the same methods across markets while tying every recommendation back to the place where you operate

## Once you pick this step, produce the deliverable together

Continuing with the travel tumbler scenario, if you decide to organize selling points first, you should finish with content you can verify and put to work. Using the fictional specs in the materials, the deliverable can include:

| What this session completes | How to use it once you have it |
|---|---|
| Comparison basis: this model is 280g, the comparison model is 320g, a 40g difference | Check the specifications and comparison conditions for both models to confirm where the claim applies |
| Usable claim: “40g lighter than the comparison model in this review” | Use as source material for product descriptions or ad copy while keeping the comparison model and source attached |
| Angle to verify: whether people who carry water daily care about this weight difference | Treat as a customer need to verify; add customer inquiries or responses before deciding whether to make it the main hook |

See the full walkthrough in the [three consulting tasks example](examples/consultant-decisions.md). When a fact changes, the method also requires updating every affected recommendation. For instance, if this tumbler's weight is corrected to 330g against the 320g comparison model, it is now 10g heavier (330 − 320 = 10). You withdraw the “40g lighter” candidate and any copy built on it, keep the updated specification and comparison source, organize new candidates from the remaining verified facts, and choose the lead angle based on evidence of customer demand

When you switch to another task, the deliverable changes with it. For campaign planning, you finish with a budget and work schedule; for one-time buyers, you finish with contact timing, a recommended second product, and a reminder plan. You can select tasks one by one from the [method index](INDEX.md), upload the full method pack with your data for that task, and complete the deliverable

That deliverable gives you copy to introduce the product and a plan you can execute. After you run it, bring back customer responses, orders, and actual costs to check how the approach performed and decide the next step. You keep the [case continuation summary](context/CASE_CONTEXT.template.md) yourself; next time, upload it alongside the method pack and updated data so the AI can build on what you have already confirmed

Through that process, you also practice reading data, understanding the reasoning behind a choice, and checking the output. The work you complete stays with you and your team, giving you verifiable records and a repeatable approach the next time a similar problem comes up

<details>
<summary><strong>How does this deliverable affect an expense? A cost comparison example</strong></summary>

Continuing with the travel tumbler task, suppose you originally received an NT$6,000 quote to outsource organizing the product information, writing the introduction, and laying out the page. Using this method, you complete the product information and draft introduction yourself; once that draft passes acceptance, the outside scope narrows to editing and layout at a revised quote of NT$2,000, with the same final deliverable and acceptance standard under both options

Suppose completing that draft adds NT$300 in tool costs and takes 4 extra hours of your time compared with the original outsourcing plan, valued at NT$300 per hour. The time valuation measures the resource you put in yourself, while cash outlay is tracked separately. All figures here are hypothetical assumptions for illustration; replace them with your own quotes and records when comparing a real job

| Comparison item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Original outsourcing plan (cash outlay) | NT$6,000 |
| Remaining outsourcing fee after completing the accepted draft yourself (cash outlay) | NT$2,000 |
| Additional tool cost (cash outlay) | NT$300 |
| Estimated value of incremental time vs. original plan: 4 hours × NT$300 (time cost) | NT$1,200 |

Cash outlay drops from NT$6,000 to NT$2,300 (2,000 + 300), a cash difference of **NT$3,700** (6,000 − 2,000 − 300 = 3,700); including the NT$1,200 valuation for the 4 incremental hours gives a difference after time cost of **NT$2,500** (6,000 − 2,000 − 300 − 1,200 = 2,500)

NT$3,700 is the reduction in cash spending, leaving those funds on hand for other uses; NT$2,500 is the cost difference after counting your added time investment. In a real assessment, check the before-and-after quotes, draft acceptance result, tool bills, and time logs item by item; if the draft needs rework, add the extra fees and hours back into the calculation before deciding which parts to handle in-house and which to outsource

</details>

## Start your first task in three steps

### 1. Download the materials and upload the index

[Download the full materials package](https://github.com/backtrue/thinkwithblack-skill/releases/latest) (for the English-only release ZIP, choose the file whose name contains `-en-`) and extract it. In an AI chat that supports file reading—such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini—upload `INDEX.md`. If you are working from the full bilingual package or repository, select `en/INDEX.md` and `en/packs/`

### 2. Bring your product and describe where things stand

Attach your product photos, specifications, product page copy, or PDF, and paste the starter prompt below. Fill in what you already know; if you are still deciding on a target market, say that directly

```text
I have this product, and I do not know what to do first. Please review the attached product information

Read the attached INDEX.md first and select the single method from the index that fits the task in front of me
If we need to confirm which product facts can be cited first, start with Product facts (mk-001)
Tell me which packs/*.md file to upload, and wait until you have read the full method pack before starting

Follow the method to organize the information we already have, state what we can complete in this session, and explain why you recommend doing this first
If I answer that I do not know, use the available evidence to propose candidate options and ways to verify them
When more information is needed, explain which judgment that piece of data will affect
Once we choose today's task, complete the deliverable according to the method and tell me how to use and verify it
When I switch to a new question later, check the index again and tell me the filename if another method pack is needed

My product and current situation: [fill in what you know, or ask the AI to read the attachments first]
Market I want to sell in: [fill in the region, or note if you are still deciding]
```

### 3. Upload the method pack and your data, then begin

From `packs/` (or `en/packs/` in the bilingual package), upload the `.md` file the AI names along with your product data, report, or draft for this task. Each method pack already combines the full step-by-step workflow, shared rules, required references, and license

If you are still preparing your own data, run your first task with the [fictional practice data](examples/README.md); if you work on the same brand regularly, fill out a [brand context template](context/MARKETING_CONTEXT.template.md) and upload it together

When you continue the same case in a later session, attach your saved [case continuation summary](context/CASE_CONTEXT.template.md); the [three consulting tasks example](examples/consultant-decisions.md) demonstrates how to choose a method, make a recommendation, and continue after a data correction

**Upload the full method pack as a file; you only need to paste the starter prompt above once per conversation.** For full operating details, see [START_HERE.md](START_HERE.md)
