# MK-093 | Organizing high- and low-season pacing


## Purpose

This skill delivers: **Seasonality hypotheses grounded in historical data**

Historical seasonality can inform planning but does not guarantee recurrence; do not create paid schedules or publish without user approval

When a seasonal ecommerce operator asks whether searches, auction competition, and conversions rise together, whether to cut budgets as end-of-season costs increase, and how to plan over the long term, use this method to examine seasonal hypotheses, events, and comparability and deliver an annual phase table. Check platform concepts against current official documentation. Treat general assumptions as hypotheses and provide method guidance without requiring account data. If the user separately requests diagnosis of an actual account, confirm the campaign type, valid outcomes, mature reporting period, and change history before handing off to the relevant method. Define brand queries separately from new-versus-existing customer identity, and platform CPA separately from new-customer CAC

## Required inputs


Available raw records: dated sales and orders by product/channel, opening time, prices/promotions, stockouts, events, and advertising

## Method Steps

1. Gather dates, revenue, orders, opening days/hours, prices/promotions, stockouts, events, and advertising for matching products/channels. Align moving holidays to the same event stage
2. Repeated directional demand changes across comparable cycles support a seasonality candidate. A single promotion or closure in one year is initially an event or supply explanation
3. Consistent demand signals after considering operating time and available products support advance inventory, production, or calendar allocation. A peak without stock or capacity calls for supply preparation before added spend
4. Without comparable history, deliver known event dates and demand scenarios rather than declaring a certain peak month. Request the matching-period record that distinguishes seasonality from an event
5. Withdraw the seasonal label when closure, stock, or price explains the change. If the direction persists but magnitude changes, update quantity and budget scenarios rather than copying last year's percentages

## Calculation and Interpretation

Report total revenue, comparable operating time, and orders/revenue per operating unit together. Normalization does not remove every difference; assess product, price, channel, and event comparability from records

Check example (hypothetical): monthly revenue falls from 300,000 to 150,000. Opening days also fall from 30 to 15, leaving 10,000 per day. Withdraw “demand halved” and plan around available operating capacity
