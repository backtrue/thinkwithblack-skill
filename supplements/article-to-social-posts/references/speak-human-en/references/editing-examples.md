# Worked English edits

These examples are fictional editorial exercises. Their numbers and scenarios are inputs for the exercise, not evidence about a real campaign

## Remove padding while keeping the evidence

Input: “In today's rapidly evolving marketing landscape, the campaign serves as a powerful testament to email's unparalleled potential, generating 18 inquiries from 240 delivered messages in June”

Edit: “The June email campaign generated 18 inquiries from 240 delivered messages”

The result preserves the supplied metric, denominator, and period. It removes the unsupported comparison with every other channel

## Preserve uncertain and conditional claims

Input: “The early results could potentially suggest that the revised checkout may have contributed to the increase, although the sample was small”

Edit: “The revised checkout may have contributed to the increase, but the early sample was small”

Keep both the uncertain attribution and sample limitation. Do not substitute “The revised checkout increased sales”

## Preserve the author's judgment

Input: “I found the setup frustrating. It took me two afternoons, and I still needed help with the export”

Edit: Keep it

A direct, specific passage needs no added personality. Do not soften the frustration into praise or invent a dramatic scene

## Explain a Taiwan case to an English reader

Source facts: A Taiwan fruit seller asked on Threads what readers would pay for a prepared fruit box. Replies named NT$70–120. No sales results were provided

Adaptation: “A fruit seller in Taiwan asked people on Threads what they would pay for a prepared fruit box. Replies suggested NT$70–120. Those replies gave the seller a starting point for a pricing test”

Keep the market and currency. Do not turn the reply range into a tested willingness-to-pay estimate, convert it to US dollars without a source, or report sales that never appeared in the source

## Keep proposals distinct from existing product features

Source proposal: Position a fruit box for office meetings, provided the seller can supply suitable portions and convenient utensils

Adaptation: “The seller could test a box for office meetings, with portions and utensils suited to sharing”

Do not write “The meeting box includes generous portions and premium utensils.” The source proposes a product configuration; it does not verify one

## Restore English subjects and relationships

Literal draft: “When doing content, first think clearly, who to see, after seeing what action, otherwise write a lot also no use”

Edit: “Before drafting, decide who the article is for and what you want that reader to do. Use those answers to choose what belongs in the article”

The edit identifies the writer and reader and reconstructs the relationship between the sentences. It does not append a new performance claim

## Keep technical meaning precise

Input: “A 100% email delivery rate means everyone saw the campaign”

For an editing-only request: identify the conflict between delivery and readership in a brief source note; do not polish it into a more persuasive false claim

For an authorized factual update: consult the reporting platform's current metric definition and replace the claim with wording supported by that source. Preserve distinctions between sent, delivered, recorded opens, clicks, and people who read the message

## Retain natural punctuation and useful repetition

Input: “I kept the original headline—the one the client had already approved. The headline was specific, and changing it would have delayed the test”

Edit: Keep it if it matches the author's sample and publication style

The dash clarifies a relevant aside. Repeating “headline” is clearer than calling it “the hook,” “the attention-grabber,” and “the lead” in successive sentences

## Do not repair one formula with another

Formulaic: “It's not about more content. It's about better content. That changes everything”

Before rewriting, find the actual point in the supplied draft. If its point is that sales questions should set the article topics, use: “Use the questions your sales team receives to choose article topics”

The replacement depends on that source fact. If no substantive point exists, remove the padding or ask for the missing point when it is essential

## End with a useful action

Input: “Ultimately, by embracing these transformative insights, marketers can unlock lasting growth in an ever-changing world”

Edit: Delete it when the preceding paragraph already finishes the argument

If the article supplied a concrete next action, end with that action. Do not manufacture a takeaway just to supply a closing paragraph
